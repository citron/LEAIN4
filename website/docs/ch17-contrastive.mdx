# Chapter 17 — Contrastive learning: the InfoNCE loss, exactly

:::tip[TL;DR]
**In short.** Contrastive methods learn an embedding by *contrasting* a positive pair against negatives. The standard loss — InfoNCE — is the cross-entropy of the question "which candidate is the positive?", so the whole cross-entropy machinery of Chapter 5 and the probability tools of Chapter 16 apply verbatim. Four theorems explain every contrastive training curve: the softmax is a genuine distribution; the loss is $\ge 0$ and vanishes only at total confidence; a **collapsed** embedding — every candidate scoring the same — loses exactly $\log (n + 1)$, so each extra negative raises the floor; and the gradient is $p - \text{onehot}$.

**New words, in simple terms:**

- **Contrastive learning**: Learn "similar" and "dissimilar" by comparing pairs: pull the positive pair together, push the negatives away.
- **InfoNCE**: The loss: classify which of the $n+1$ candidates is the positive, and pay the negative log of the probability you assign to the right answer.
- **Temperature** $\tau$: Dividing the scores by $\tau$ sets how peaked the softmax is: small $\tau$ amplifies small score differences.
- **Representation collapse**: All embeddings become (nearly) identical; every candidate ties; the loss sits exactly at $\log (n+1)$.
:::

Chapter 15 penalises collapse in JEPA with a variance term but never proves what the *loss* of a collapsed model must be — because contrastive learning was not in the course. That gap is this chapter: the loss itself, defined over exact `Rat`, with the four facts every practitioner quotes as theorems, not folklore.

## The loss

```lean file=Ch17/Contrastive.lean#infonce
```

A sample is a query, a positive, and $n$ negatives; the score of candidate $i$ is the product with the query (a cosine in practice — a plain product keeps everything exact). InfoNCE is the negative log of the softmax mass on index 0, the positive. The whole file is `Rat` and the abstract laws of `Ch16` (`LawfulLog`, `LawfulExp`): the statements are theorems, not plots.

Four things the block above proves:

**The softmax is a distribution** (`softmax_dist`): every mass is *strictly* positive, and the masses sum to exactly 1. The proof needs `vsum_pos` — the partition function $Z = \sum_i e^{z_i}$ is strictly positive as soon as there is one score — so the division is legal, no mass is zero, and $\log$ of every mass is defined. The `Dist` record of Chapter 16 accepts the softmax as input: cross-entropy, entropy and KL all apply to it.

**The loss is nonnegative** (`infoNce_ge_zero`): the positive's numerator $e^{z_0}$ is at most the whole $Z$ (the other terms are positive), so the mass is $\le 1$, and the tangent law $\log x \le x - 1$ gives $-\log p \ge 0$.

**Collapse costs exactly $\log (n+1)$** (`infoNce_collapse`): give every candidate the same score $c \ne 0$ and the loss is exactly $\log (n + 1)$ — the uniform mass $1/(n+1)$, no approximation. This is the number to read off any training curve: *chance* gives the same baseline, $\log(n+1)$, because the uniform distribution is the classifier that knows nothing. More negatives raise the floor — exercise 1 computes the marginal cost of the next negative, $\log\frac{n+2}{n+1}$.

**The gradient is $p - \text{onehot}$** (`infonce_grad`): InfoNCE *is* Chapter 5's cross-entropy loss with a one-hot label on index 0, so its gradient is inherited from `ceLoss_grad` in one line: the gradient on the logit of candidate $i$ is its current mass $p_i$, minus $1$ for the positive. Each candidate is pushed with exactly the probability mass it should give up: a negative that grabbed $60\,\%$ of the mass is pushed away the hardest; a negative already at $0.01\,\%$ is barely touched. This one identity explains the whole dynamics of contrastive training — and why hard negatives (mass $=$ big) dominate the signal.

## The matched pair, in closed form

```lean file=Ch17/Contrastive.lean#matched
```

Strip the batch to one query, one positive (score `hi`) and one negative (score `lo`) and InfoNCE stops being an aggregate: it collapses to a single elementary function of the **similarity gap** `hi - lo`. The block above proves the collapse, exactly:

**The positive's mass is a sigmoid** (`matched_mass`): `1 / (1 + exp(lo - hi))`. The key step is `matched_key` — `(1 + exp(lo-hi)) · exp(hi) = exp(hi) + exp(lo)`, one application of `exp_add`. So the mass is `1` when the positive dominates, exactly `1/2` when the model is blind (`hi = lo`), and tends to `0` when the pair is scored backwards. Every contrastive paper's "accuracy" plot is this curve.

**The loss is a softplus** (`matched_loss`): `log (1 + exp(lo - hi))`. The loss is a *pure function of the gap* — the individual scores do not matter, only their difference. This is why temperature and score scale fight each other in every real run: they are the same dial.

**Blindness costs exactly `log 2`** (`matched_equal`): with `hi = lo` the loss is `log 2` and the mass is `1/2`, not approximately. For `n` negatives the same statement is `infoNce_collapse` above; this is its two-candidate shadow.

**The loss decreases as the gap grows** (`matched_mono`) — and this one is honest about its hypothesis: monotonicity of `exp` is an *analytic* property of the real exponential. It does **not** follow from `exp_add` and `exp_zero` alone — any group homomorphism `(Q,+) → (Q\{0},·)` satisfies those laws, and wild ones make `exp` decreasing. The theorem therefore asks for the class `MonotoneExp` — a single named declaration in `Core/Algebra.lean` carrying exactly one field, `a ≤ b → exp a ≤ exp b`. The real exponential satisfies it; the algebraic layer alone cannot prove it. Declaring the analytic frontier **once**, as a class, instead of repeating a local hypothesis at each use site, is part of the formalisation: every theorem that needs analysis points to the same named door.

The proof also derives `log_mono` — logarithm monotone on the positives — from the tangent bound `log t ≤ t - 1` applied to the ratio `x / y` (`log_div`). No new axiom: a theorem of the four `LawfulLog` laws.

## Experiments

`lake exe ch17` prints the numeric face of the four theorems. On `Float`:

- a softmax of five logits sums to `1.000000` (the theorem says *exactly* 1; `Float` says it to the last bit it can keep);
- a flat embedding of $n+1$ candidates scores `1.39 / 2.08 / 2.77` for $n = 3, 7, 15$ negatives, against $\log (n+1) = 1.39, 2.08, 2.77$ — gap `0.00` at every size: the floor is not a metaphor, it is arithmetic;
- a discriminative embedding (score 4 on the positive, 0 on the four negatives) loses `0.07` where the floor is `1.61`: the loss *can* go under the floor — that is what learning is;
- the gradient `[-0.77, 0.05, 0.01, 0.62, 0.08]` sums to 0, with the positive's entry negative: pushed up, and the massiest negative pushed down the hardest.

Then a real training run: five items on a circle, fixed queries at radius 1.5, embeddings on the unit sphere, cosine scores at temperature $\tau = 0.5$, plain SGD with a projection back to the sphere after every step (an unnormalised embedding runs away — the classic contrastive blow-up, caught in the open). The run *starts at the collapse floor*: every score is 1.5, the loss is $\log 5 = 1.61$ exactly. After 500 epochs the mean loss is **0.24**, and it stays at $0.23$–$0.25$ to epoch 2000; the mean cosine between each learned embedding and its target item is **1.00**. The embedding did not merely beat the floor — it recovered the items.

Around tau = 1 the curve is steepest - which is why the temperature is the single most-tuned hyper-parameter of every contrastive run." />

## What is and is not covered

- **A sample, not a batch.** The theorems are about one query with $n$ negatives. Real runs pool negatives across a batch (the SupCon / SimCLR layout); the loss is then a sum of $n+1$-candidate losses, and every theorem here applies to each row.
- **Scores are products, not cosines.** Exact cosines need square roots and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality — analysis-adjacent. A product keeps the model finite and rational; the demo does the cosine in `Float`.
- **No Information-Maximising bound.** The name "InfoNCE" comes from a bound on mutual information; proving it needs measure-theoretic entropy. What this chapter proves is the loss's *skeleton*: floor, sign, gradient — the parts every curve shows.
- **Temperature is a parameter, not a theory.** The theorems hold for every $\tau > 0$; the empirical literature on temperature (it interacts with the number of negatives, the batch, the projection head) is experiment, not theorem.
