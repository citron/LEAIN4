# Chapter 10.5 — Boltzmann machines and contrastive divergence

:::tip[TL;DR]
**In short.** A Boltzmann machine defines a distribution by assigning an *energy* to every configuration: probability is proportional to $\mathrm e^{-E}$. We prove, on the exact canonical machine (one visible bit, one hidden bit, rational arithmetic), that the Gibbs distribution is normalised, that its conditionals are the Boltzmann conditionals, and that **one full Gibbs sweep leaves the equilibrium measure invariant** - the theorem behind contrastive divergence.

**New words, in simple terms:**

- **Energy-based model**: A model that scores configurations by an energy; low energy means likely.
- **Partition function $Z$**: The sum of $\mathrm e^{-E}$ over all configurations; it turns energies into probabilities.
- **Gibbs distribution**: The unique distribution with mass $\mathrm e^{-E(s)} / Z$ on each state.
- **Restricted Boltzmann machine (RBM)**: A bipartite Boltzmann machine: visible units, hidden units, no links inside a layer.
- **Gibbs sweep**: Alternate updates: redraw all hidden units given the visible, then all visible given the hidden.
- **Contrastive divergence**: Train by running one (cheap) sweep instead of the infinite one the exact gradient asks for.
:::

<Creators slugs="ackley-hinton-sejnowski,hinton,lehriche-pinto-hinton" />

The VAE gives you a generator with an *explicit* latent code; the GAN gives you a generator trained by an *adversary*. There is a third, older family: models with **no generator network at all**, where the distribution itself is the model, written directly as

$$ p(v, h) \;=\; \frac{\mathrm e^{-E(v,h)}}{Z}, \qquad Z = \sum_{v',h'} \mathrm e^{-E(v',h')}. $$

This is the **Boltzmann machine** (Ackley, Hinton and Sejnowski, 1985), after the spin glasses of statistical physics. It is the direct ancestor of modern deep learning - Hinton's 2006 dream of stacking RBMs launched the deep learning boom - and its generative cousin, contrastive divergence, is what made them trainable.

## The canonical machine, exactly

One visible bit $v$, one hidden bit $h$. The energy has a bias $a$ for the hidden unit, a bias $b$ for the visible unit and a coupling $g$ for the pair being on together; the gauge is $E(0,0)=0$. `w_sum` proves the normalisation on $\mathbb{Q}$: the four masses $u(s)/Z$ sum to exactly $1$. Every probability in this chapter is a `Rat` computed from exact arithmetic - no floating point is involved in any proof.

## The Gibbs conditionals

On a bipartite graph the conditionals are local: given the visible unit, the hidden unit is a coin with odds

$$ \frac{p(h=1 \mid v)}{p(h=0 \mid v)} \;=\; \frac{u(1,v)}{u(0,v)}, $$

and symmetrically for the visible. This is why the RBM sweeps so cheaply: conditioning on one layer makes the other layer *independent*.

The module proves `qh_pos` / `qh_sum` (the conditionals are genuine probabilities) and the **odds-ratio identities** `odds_hidden_on` / `odds_hidden_off`: sampling parameters are exactly ratios of weights - the mechanism contrastive divergence exploits.

## One sweep leaves equilibrium alone: the theorem

The Gibbs chain: from a visible state $v$, draw $h \sim p(h \mid v)$, then draw $v' \sim p(v' \mid h)$. The deep fact - the finite shadow of the ergodic theorem for Gibbs chains - is that **the Gibbs distribution is invariant under one full sweep**. The module proves it state by state, in exact rational arithmetic, for the canonical machine:

```lean file=Ch105/Boltzmann.lean#sweep_invariance
```

The proof factorises the sweep kernel,

$$ S(s, t) \;=\; p(h_t \mid v_s)\; p(v_t \mid h_t), $$

and reduces invariance to two families of **marginal identities** (`therm_hidden` and `gibbs_factor`): thermalising one layer from equilibrium reproduces exactly that layer's equilibrium marginal. No limits, no mixing times - for a finite machine this is algebra.

## The energy form

Writing $a = \mathrm e^{\alpha}$, $b = \mathrm e^{\beta}$, $g = \mathrm e^{\delta}$, the weights are exponentials of an energy,

$$ E(v,h) \;=\; -\alpha\, h \;-\; \beta\, v \;-\; \delta\, v h, $$

and the module proves the **Boltzmann form** $u(s) = \mathrm e^{-E(s)}$ and the partition identity $Z = \sum_s \mathrm e^{-E(s)}$ on any lawful exponential (`LawfulExp`), with the positivity of $\mathrm e^{x}$ proved from the square trick $\mathrm e^{x} = (\mathrm e^{x/2})^2$:


This is the bridge from the combinatorial model to statistical physics: the weights *are* Boltzmann factors, so the whole machine is the canonical ensemble of a two-spin system, and free energy $\log Z$ is a loss the model implicitly minimises.

## From invariance to contrastive divergence

The exact gradient of $\log p(v)$ is

$$ \nabla_\theta \log p(v) \;=\; \mathbb E_{p(\cdot \mid v)}[\nabla_\theta E] \;-\; \mathbb E_{p}[\nabla_\theta E], $$

a *positive* phase at the data and a *negative* phase at the model equilibrium. The negative phase is intractable in general: it needs the thermalised chain. **Contrastive divergence** (Hinton, 2002) runs the chain for $k$ steps from the data instead of to equilibrium, and the `sweep_invariance` theorem is exactly what justifies the trick at $k \to \infty$: a chain that has thermalised samples from the model, and one sweep is the minimum work that keeps it there. At $k = 1$ (CD-1) you pay one sweep per gradient; Hinton's argument - and the theorem above - is that the parameters barely move, so the chain barely drifts away from equilibrium, and one sweep keeps it close.

Where this chapter sits in the course:

- **Chapter 10 (VAE)**: an *encoder-based* generator with an explicit latent; the RBM has no encoder - inference is one exact conditional sweep, proved above.
- **Chapter 11 (GAN)**: a generator trained by an adversary; the Boltzmann machine is trained by *free energy*, no adversary.
- **Chapters 17 / 19 (contrastive, multimodal)**: InfoNCE contrasts positive and negative pairs; contrastive divergence contrasts positive and negative *phases of the same energy* - the same shape of idea, one generation earlier.

:::warning What is proved here, and what is not
Proved, exactly and over `Rat`: the normalisation of the canonical 4-state machine, the Gibbs conditionals, the invariance of equilibrium under one full Gibbs sweep (state by state, coupling by coupling), and the energy form of the joint law. Not proved — and out of this model's scope: that *many* sweeps mix (no mixing-time theory here), that the results extend beyond four states to real RBMs with many hidden units, that contrastive divergence trains a realistic RBM, and anything about sampling quality at scale. The toy is small on purpose: it is the smallest machine where every claim is a closed identity.
:::
